Which orders are going to arrive late?

I took about 100k real orders from Olist, a Brazilian marketplace (2016 to 2018), and built the whole chain: cleaning, a SQL database with delivery KPIs, a model that flags risky orders at purchase time, and monthly monitoring of that model.

On-time rate by purchase month

Two bad stretches: November 2017 (Black Friday volume, 87.6% on time) and March 2018 (81.0%). Most months sit between 93 and 97%.

Distance vs late rate, by customer state

About 71% of orders ship from São Paulo sellers, so northeastern states like Alagoas (21% late) and Maranhão (17%) are hit hardest. Distance explains a lot, but not everything: Rio is only ~490 km away and still runs 12% late.

What I found

  • Late deliveries wreck reviews. 62% of late orders got a 1 or 2 star review, compared with 9% of on-time ones (Mann-Whitney and chi-square both p < 0.001).
  • Distance matters. Spearman correlation between distance and delivery time is 0.54.
  • Payments mostly reconcile. Only 313 of 98,666 orders differ from items + freight by more than 1 BRL, mostly installment interest.

The late-delivery model

Trained on 2017, tested on January to August 2018 (52,777 orders, 7.7% late). It only uses what's known at purchase time, and the history features only look at deliveries that were already finished, so there's no leakage.

Monitoring the model month by month

In February and March 2018 the real late rate jumped to 14 and 19% while the model still predicted 7 to 8%. It kept ranking orders well (ROC AUC 0.67 to 0.71), so the flag list stayed useful, but this is exactly the signal to recalibrate or retrain.
